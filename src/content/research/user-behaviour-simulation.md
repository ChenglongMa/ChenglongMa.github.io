A user simulator makes claims about behaviour through its inputs, observations, actions, and outputs. I study how these elements can be made inspectable and evaluated against relevant human evidence. This includes defining what information a simulated user may access, recording evidence before actions, and comparing the resulting interaction with the behaviour the model is intended to represent.

My current questions concern process and outcome validation, responses to interface changes, and the use of human process information in downstream prediction. Agreement on one measure does not establish validity for another use. I therefore examine the target quantity, measurement, configuration, and applicable conditions behind each evaluation claim.

### Current questions

- Which aspects of an interaction process are informative about later behaviour?
- When do process-informed updates improve downstream estimates?
- How does a simulator’s information access affect the conclusions it can support?
- What human evidence is needed when moving to a new task or population?
